\chapter{Testing and Evaluation}
\label{ch:evaluation}

% BUDGET: 7 pages.

Evaluation here operates at three levels: component tests that establish each
deterministic mechanism does what it claims, a frozen benchmark that scores the
code-generation stage repeatedly under controlled conditions, and end-to-end runs
that exercise all seven agents on real research questions. A fourth, the human
evaluation, assesses whether the output is usable by the people the system is
for.

One theme runs through the first three and is stated here because it shaped what
the later sections could claim: on this project, building trustworthy measurement
proved consistently harder than building the thing being measured. Three of the
findings below are defects in the evaluation apparatus rather than in the
pipeline, and in each case the apparatus was reporting the system as worse than
it was.

\section{Component testing}
\label{sec:component-testing}

The suite comprises 1{,}190 tests across 33 files. Its organising principle is
NFR5: all non-model logic must be testable without a language model present.
Table~\ref{tab:deterministic-modules} lists the modules this applies to --- some
4{,}900 lines of logic that runs with no model, no network and no cluster.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\caption{Deterministic modules, all tested with no language model present.}
\label{tab:deterministic-modules}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lrX}
\toprule
\textbf{Module} & \textbf{Lines} & \textbf{Responsibility} \\
\midrule
\texttt{coder/sandbox} & 1862 & environment probes, venv provisioning, execution, static checks \\
\texttt{coder/provenance} & 774 & resolving inputs to real or surrogate; withholding the verdict \\
\texttt{coder/repair} & 342 & the repairs needing no model call \\
\texttt{coder/diagnose} & 317 & classifying a failure by kind \\
\texttt{writer/citations} & 337 & citation resolution and disambiguation \\
\texttt{reviewer/checks} & 330 & citation, results-accuracy and coverage checking \\
\texttt{literature/nodes} & 386 & deduplication, screening, merging \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

Two conventions make this possible. Every environment dependency is injected
through a constructor rather than patched: tests pass
\texttt{network\_check=lambda: False} and a fake dataset lookup instead of
monkeypatching four HTTP endpoints. And the modules above read no settings and
call no model, so they behave identically on a laptop and a compute node.

Tests are written against observed failures wherever one exists. The regression
tests for verdict normalisation use the exact values a benchmark run recorded;
those for the citation checker use the exact reference-list line that produced
thousands of spurious issues. This keeps the suite a record of what actually went
wrong rather than a catalogue of what might.

\section{The Coder benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}

The Coder Agent is scored against a frozen corpus of twelve experiment plans
spanning tabular classification, regression, text classification, clustering,
time series, deep learning on a GPU, Monte Carlo simulation, bootstrap
resampling, an infeasible plan that should be refused, and a restricted clinical
source that should be declined. The headline metric is \emph{interpretable}: an
experiment counts only if it ran \emph{and} carries a real boolean verdict.
Completion alone is not enough, because an experiment that finishes on
synthesised data has produced numbers without evidence.

Nine runs were collected on identical inputs.

\subsection{The benchmark does not support single-run comparison}
\label{sec:variance}

The five runs under the system's default configuration returned interpretable
scores of \textbf{4, 6, 3, 2 and 3} out of twelve. Completions were far more
stable at 10, 11, 10, 10 and 10, and the count of experiments resolving to real
data varied from 4 to 8.

A threefold spread on identical plans, identical code and the same model means
that a difference of one or two between two runs carries no information. This
matters beyond a caveat, because the benchmark harness ends every run by printing
an invitation to compare it against another single run. The instrument actively
encourages an inference its own variance does not support. Every comparison in
the remainder of this chapter therefore reports the range across repeated runs,
and no claim rests on a single pair.

The variance is not itself a defect. Each stage of the pipeline makes many model
calls, and the corpus is small enough that one experiment changing category moves
the score by 8 percentage points. What was a defect was the absence of repetition
until late in the project: several earlier conclusions about whether a change had
helped were drawn from single runs and could not have been sound.

\subsection{A defect in the measurement, not the system}

One run scored 4 interpretable when the correct figure was 6. Cases 05 and 12 had
each computed a genuine verdict which was recorded as the \emph{string}
\texttt{"True"} or \texttt{"False"} rather than a boolean, and the scorer's strict
identity test rejected both.

The cause is a type crossing a boundary unvalidated. Generated \texttt{evaluate()}
code computes its verdict from a comparison over numpy values, which yields
\texttt{numpy.bool\_}; that is not JSON-serialisable, so the template's
\texttt{json.dump(\ldots, default=str)} stringified it. Downstream,
\texttt{compute\_hypothesis\_verdict} tests \texttt{meets is True}, which a string
fails, so a correct result was silently reported as inconclusive.

It failed safe --- no false claim was ever made, because the strict comparison is
precisely the discipline Chapter~\ref{ch:design} argues for --- but real results
were discarded, and the project's own instrument under-reported its own
improvement. The fix normalises at the origin, where the type is still known,
rather than relaxing the downstream comparison whose strictness is the safety
property. It parses rather than casts, since \texttt{bool("False")} is
\texttt{True} and casting would have converted a refutation into a confirmation.
Results below report both the recorded and the corrected figures.

\section{An ablation: dataset discovery}
\label{sec:ablation}

Section~\ref{sec:wrong-data} shows the dataset discovery mechanism attaching
irrelevant data to experiments. The natural question is whether the pipeline
would be more trustworthy without it. Following the ablation methodology
AutoMETA~\cite{lee2026autometa} applies to its own protocol enforcement, discovery
was disabled and the frozen corpus re-run four times.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\caption{Dataset discovery enabled versus disabled, on identical plans.
Mean with observed range; ranges rather than standard deviations, since four to
five runs cannot support an interval estimate.}
\label{tab:ablation}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Xcc}
\toprule
& \textbf{Discovery on} ($n{=}5$) & \textbf{Discovery off} ($n{=}4$) \\
\midrule
Completed                  & 10.2 [10--11] & 7.2 [7--8] \\
Interpretable (corrected)  & 3.6 [2--6]    & 0.0 [0--0] \\
Real data only             & 5.0 [4--8]    & 0.2 [0--1] \\
Fix attempts               & 14.6 [12--17] & 36.0 [34--41] \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The interpretable column must not be read as a quality difference. With discovery
off, every resolved input becomes a surrogate, and a surrogate withholds the
verdict by design, so a score of zero is what the provenance mechanism is
\emph{supposed} to produce when no real data exists. Comparing the arms on that
metric is close to tautological.

The informative comparison is throughput, and there the result is unambiguous.
Experiments that never ran at all rose from 2 to 8, and repair effort rose from
14.6 fix attempts to 36.0 --- two and a half times the work for fewer finished
experiments. Offering the model one concrete dataset, with real column names and
real sample rows, is load-bearing for its ability to write working code,
independently of whether that dataset is the right one.

This is the opposite of the expected result, and it settles the design question
the next section raises. Disabling discovery is not the remedy for wrong data: it
costs roughly a third of completions and more than doubles the repair effort while
producing nothing interpretable at all. The remedy has to be stricter matching,
not removal.

\section{Wrong-but-real data}
\label{sec:wrong-data}

The most serious failure this evaluation found is not an experiment that breaks.
It is one that succeeds on the wrong data.

Across both configurations of the frozen corpus, the Hugging Face dataset
\texttt{google/Synthetic-Persona-Chat} --- a corpus of generated dialogue --- was
resolved as the input for \emph{six of the twelve cases}, including clustering,
bootstrap resampling, Monte Carlo simulation and tabular classification. It is
relevant to none of them. The mechanism is visible in the run logs: the search
matched on the literal phrase \emph{``hugging face''} taken from a data
requirement that named no subject. The same behaviour had previously matched a
requirement reading \emph{``public dataset''} to a corpus of public-domain poetry.

The consequence is worse than a surrogate. Every one of those inputs was labelled
\texttt{real\_local} or \texttt{real\_download}, so the provenance mechanism ---
which withholds a verdict only when data was \emph{synthesised} --- had nothing to
object to, and the verdict was asserted. In one case the generated code recorded
in its own \texttt{assumptions\_made} that it had used a web-crawl corpus
\emph{``as a proxy for movie review sentiment corpus''} and derived its labels by
keyword because the corpus had none, and then reported the hypothesis
\emph{supported}.

A surrogate withholds a verdict; wrong real data claims one. This is the
over-claiming direction the entire provenance design exists to prevent, arriving
through a route it did not cover.

Two responses were implemented. The model had \emph{declared} what it did, in
\texttt{assumptions\_made}, and nothing read the declaration; a deterministic pass
now does, withholding the verdict when the text asserts a substitution while
ignoring the conditional fallback the prompt requires. Separately, an input that
was \emph{discovered} rather than named is now marked as needing confirmation
before its verdict stands, on the grounds that a keyword match is not evidence
that a dataset answers a question. Neither closes the case where the model
substitutes silently, which cannot be caught by reading prose.

\section{End-to-end runs}
\label{sec:end-to-end}

Thirty-six interdisciplinary research questions were run through the complete
pipeline, spanning finance, health, climate, economics, language and the physical
sciences. All thirty-six completed, producing 108 drafted papers across three
writer/reviewer iterations each, with a mean of 3.9 cross-field bridge insights
per run. An earlier sweep of twenty questions had completed three; the difference
is attributable to question design --- each of the thirty-six names a concrete
data type and an explicit baseline --- and to hardware that left a GPU free for
the experiments themselves.

At the Coder stage, 25 of 36 experiments completed and 11 generated code that was
not run. Of the completed ones, 9 reported a hypothesis supported, 7 refuted, and
9 had their verdict withheld.

\subsection{A verification mechanism that failed by over-reporting}

Every one of the 36 runs exhausted its three writer/reviewer iterations without
passing review, and mean issue counts \emph{rose} across iterations, from 34.8 to
36.0 to 36.8. Revision improved 17 runs, worsened 17 and left 2 unchanged.

The cause was a false-positive storm in the deterministic citation check.
Table~\ref{tab:citation-issues} gives the breakdown.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering\small
\caption{Review issues across 36 runs and 108 drafts.}
\label{tab:citation-issues}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Xr}
\toprule
\textbf{Issue source} & \textbf{Count} \\
\midrule
Citation issues located in the References section & 3{,}514 \\
Citation issues anywhere else & 4 \\
All other categories (results accuracy, hypothesis coverage) & 353 \\
\midrule
\textbf{Total} & \textbf{3{,}871} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The checker was scanning the paper's own reference list. Entries render as
\emph{``Riccardo Alberti and Sven Karbach (2026). Title\ldots''}, so the
narrative-citation pattern captured the \emph{last} author's surname before the
year, while the lookup is keyed on the \emph{first}. Every multi-author reference
therefore failed to resolve and was reported as possibly fabricated. A single
paper produced 55 spurious issues from its reference list against 44
citation-shaped matches in its entire body.

Two conclusions follow. The Writer's citation grounding was working essentially
perfectly: four genuine citation issues across 108 drafts. And the failure mode of
a verification mechanism matters as much as its coverage --- by over-reporting,
this check made \texttt{overall\_pass} unreachable, rendered the revision loop
inert, and made a guarantee that \emph{was} holding impossible to confirm. A
checker that cries wolf is not merely noisy; it destroys the signal it exists to
provide. Reference entries are rendered mechanically from the retrieved paper
index and so cannot be fabricated by construction; they are now excluded.
