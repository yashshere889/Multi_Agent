\chapter{Conclusion and Future Work}
\label{ch:conclusion}

% BUDGET: 2 pages. Closes every aim opened in Ch.1.

This project set out to let a researcher move from a question to a
literature-grounded draft paper without writing code, and to do so on
institutional infrastructure without ever presenting a claim that cannot be
checked. A seven-agent pipeline was delivered against that aim, every agent
implemented as a graph so that each model call and each deterministic check is an
addressable step (C1); a reliability architecture that classifies a failure before
responding to it and makes a failed attempt cheap before making it rare (C3); a
provenance mechanism that withholds a verdict rather than assert one on invented
data (C4); and a preference dataset assembled from the system's own fix loop, in
which the rejecting check supplies the label (C6). Chapter~\ref{ch:evaluation}
assesses each aim individually against the runs collected.

The result I would put first is not the pipeline. It is the catalogue of places an
end-to-end research system turns out to need verification, each paired with the
failure that motivated it (C2), together with the finding that motivated the last
third of the project: \emph{a guardrail's correctness and its coverage are separate
properties, and only the second can be measured from a run}. Three mechanisms in
this system were correct in the file and inert in production --- a confirmation
gate that never fired, a staged-file matcher that answered any requirement
containing the word ``dataset'', and a citation check that reported thousands of
fabrications where there were four. Each passed its own tests. Each was found by
reading what a sweep actually did, and the empirical account of them (C5) is the
part of this work most likely to transfer to another system.

The system's honest standing is given by its last sweep: thirty-four of thirty-six
questions produced a complete drafted paper, twenty-six experiments ran, and every
one of those had its verdict withheld. Nothing was over-claimed because nothing was
claimed. That is the correct behaviour for data nobody has confirmed, and it is
also the clearest statement of what remains to be built.

\section{Future work}
\label{sec:future-overlap}

\paragraph{The human evaluation, and the infrastructure it needs.} The largest gap
in this dissertation's evidence is that no researcher has used the system
(Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). The study is designed and its participants were
willing: each enters a research question in their own field, drives the pipeline
through the web interface, and reviews the drafted paper, with the instrument asking
the three questions the requirements analysis raised --- whether the draft is usable,
whether the reader understands what the system withheld and why, and whether their
trust in it is calibrated to what it actually verified.

What blocked it is scheduling, not design, and it is worth recording because any
group deploying such a tool on institutional hardware will meet it. A live session
needs a GPU at an agreed hour on a facility that allocates by queue and pre-empts on
its most available partitions. Two routes around it are open. The first is to
reserve compute for the session window --- an interactive allocation or a booked
node, which the facility supports on request --- so that the model server is up
before the participant arrives. The second decouples the participant from the
compute entirely: pre-run a set of questions, then have the participant drive the
interface over completed runs, inspecting stage outputs, provenance records and
drafts as artefacts rather than waiting for them. The second is weaker evidence about
the experience of \emph{running} the system and stronger evidence about whether its
output can be understood and trusted, which is the more important of the two
questions and the one this project cannot currently answer at all.

\paragraph{Overlap detection (D6).} Both requirements-analysis respondents rated
the flagging of overlap with existing literature 5 out of 5 --- the only item to
score the maximum from both --- and Gupta and Pruthi's finding that a substantial
share of agent-generated research documents contain undetected
plagiarism~\cite{gupta2025glitters} makes the omission a matter of research
integrity rather than convenience. It is the project's most significant unmet
requirement. The mechanism it needs is within reach of the existing architecture:
the Writer already resolves every claim against an indexed corpus, so the same
index supports a similarity pass over drafted passages, and the deterministic
half --- near-duplicate detection against retrieved papers --- requires no model at
all. What it additionally needs, and what could not be assembled in the time
available, is comparison against a corpus far larger than the twenty to fifty
papers a single run retrieves.

\paragraph{Confirmation, which is now the binding constraint.} Sweep~3 withheld
every verdict it produced, ten of them because the data was real but found by
keyword search rather than named by anyone. The missing step is small and belongs
in the interface that already exists: present the discovered dataset, its landing
page and its first rows, and let the user confirm or reject it before the verdict
stands. This fits the stage-selection model D5 established and would convert the
system's most common withheld outcome into a verdict a reader can trust ---
without weakening any gate, because the confirmation would come from a person
rather than from a keyword match.

\paragraph{Checking the plan, not only the code.} The metric-saturation gate of
Section~\ref{sec:saturation} withholds verdicts from experiments that were
implemented correctly from an unsound design --- a label derived from one of the
model's own inputs, or two arms that cannot differ. Withholding is the safe
response, but the defect originates in the Experiment Planner, and catching it
there, before code is generated, would recover results rather than discard them.
The same applies to a narrower case sweep~3 exposed: a plan naming a
\emph{repository} (``UCI ML Repository'') rather than a dataset is currently taken
literally by the search.

\paragraph{What the respondents asked for and did not get.} Confidence or
uncertainty estimates, and compute cost and time estimates, were wanted by every
respondent and are absent. Both are display problems over data the pipeline
already holds --- the provenance and compute-provenance documents record what was
reduced, what did not converge and what was withheld --- and neither is a research
problem.

\paragraph{Preference optimisation at scale.} Chapter~\ref{ch:po} demonstrates that
the fix loop yields learnable preference data, on 121 pairs and a small model held
out from the pipeline. The honest next step is on-policy pairs from the served
model and an evaluation against downstream experiment success rather than held-out
preference accuracy.

\bigskip

What this system is, at the end of the project, is a research assistant that will
carry a question through literature, hypothesis, experiment and draft without
asking its user to write code, and that refuses to tell them a result it cannot
justify. The refusals are not a shortfall in that ambition. They are the part of it
I would least want to remove.
